### Answers

#### 220. What is BGP? [D | ML]
* **Definition:** Border Gateway Protocol (BGP) is the path-vector routing protocol that runs the global Internet's backbone, exchanging reachability information between different Autonomous Systems (AS) — independently administered networks like ISPs, large enterprises, and content providers.
* **Key Note:** BGP is the only protocol designed and trusted to operate at Internet scale, connecting hundreds of thousands of networks worldwide.

#### 221. What is the difference between an IGP and an EGP? [D | ML]
* **IGP (Interior Gateway Protocol):** Routes traffic *within* a single Autonomous System (e.g., OSPF, EIGRP) — optimized for fast convergence and detailed topology awareness.
* **EGP (Exterior Gateway Protocol):** Routes traffic *between* different Autonomous Systems (BGP is effectively the only EGP in modern use) — optimized for policy control and stability at massive scale, not raw speed.

#### 222. What is the difference between eBGP and iBGP? [D | ML]
* **eBGP (External BGP):** A BGP session between routers in **different** Autonomous Systems — typically the connection between an ISP and its customer, or between two ISPs (peering).
* **iBGP (Internal BGP):** A BGP session between routers within the **same** Autonomous System, used to carry externally learned BGP routes consistently across an organization's internal network.

#### 223. Why is BGP called a "path-vector" protocol? [D | ML]
* **Definition:** BGP advertises the complete list of Autonomous Systems (the AS-PATH) a route has traversed, rather than just a simple distance (like RIP) or a full topology (like OSPF).
* **Purpose:** This AS-PATH information allows routers to detect and reject routing loops simply by checking whether their own AS number already appears in the path, and gives administrators policy-based control over path selection.

#### 224. What transport protocol and port does BGP use, and why does that matter? [D | ML]
* **Transport:** BGP runs over **TCP port 179**, unlike most IGPs which use IP directly or UDP.
* **Why It Matters:** Because it rides on TCP, BGP inherits reliable, ordered delivery and doesn't need to build its own reliability mechanisms — but it also means firewall rules and ACLs affecting TCP 179 directly impact whether BGP peers can establish a session.

#### 225. What is an AS-PATH attribute used for? [D | ML]
* **Definition:** A BGP path attribute listing every Autonomous System number a route advertisement has passed through on its way to the current router.
* **Uses:** Loop prevention (a router rejects any route containing its own AS number) and path selection (a shorter AS-PATH is generally preferred, all else being equal).

#### 226. Why does BGP prioritize path selection differently than IGPs like OSPF? [D | ML]
* **Explanation:** OSPF picks the path with the lowest technical cost (based on bandwidth). BGP instead runs through a long, ordered list of **policy-based attributes** (such as Local Preference, AS-PATH length, Origin type, and MED) before ever considering something like bandwidth — because at Internet scale, business relationships, contracts, and traffic-engineering policy matter more than raw link speed.

#### 227. Configure a basic eBGP peering between two routers in AS 65001 and AS 65002. [S | ML]
```
! Router A (AS 65001)
RouterA(config)# router bgp 65001
RouterA(config-router)# neighbor 203.0.113.2 remote-as 65002

! Router B (AS 65002)
RouterB(config)# router bgp 65002
RouterB(config-router)# neighbor 203.0.113.1 remote-as 65001
```
* **Verification:** `show ip bgp summary` on either router to confirm the neighbor state shows an established session (a numeric prefix count, not a state name like "Idle" or "Active").

#### 228. Two routers are not forming a BGP neighbor relationship. What do you check? [T | ML]
1. **IP Reachability:** Confirm the two neighbor IP addresses can reach each other (ping), since BGP requires underlying IP connectivity — often via a static or IGP route first.
2. **Remote-AS Mismatch:** Verify the configured `remote-as` number on each side matches the actual AS number of its peer.
3. **TCP Port 179 Blocked:** Check for an ACL or firewall blocking TCP port 179 between the two routers.
4. **Authentication Mismatch:** If MD5 authentication is configured, confirm both sides use the identical password.
5. **State Check:** Run `show ip bgp summary` — a state stuck at "Active" or "Idle" (instead of an established prefix count) confirms the session isn't forming and points to one of the above.

#### 229. Why would an ISP support engineer need basic BGP awareness even in an L1/L2 role? [S | ML]
* **Explanation:** While L1/L2 engineers rarely configure BGP directly, understanding it helps in recognizing symptoms during escalations — for example, knowing that a customer's public IP reachability issue might be caused by a route being withdrawn or not being advertised upstream by the ISP's BGP routers, which helps frame a clearer, more accurate escalation to L3/NOC engineering teams rather than assuming it's a local Layer 1/2 issue.

#### 230. Explain BGP to a non-technical customer using an analogy. [N | EL]
* **Analogy:** "Think of BGP like international shipping companies agreeing on trade routes between countries. Each country (network) advertises which destinations it can deliver to and through which partner countries, and shippers choose routes based on agreements and reliability — not always the shortest physical distance. That's how your data finds its way across dozens of different companies' networks to reach a website on the other side of the world."
